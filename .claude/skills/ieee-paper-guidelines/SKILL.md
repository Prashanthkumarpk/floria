# IEEE Conference Paper Guidelines — Validation Skill

Source: IEEE conference paper template ("Preparation of a Formatted Conference Paper for an
IEEE Power & Energy Society Conference", the standard IEEE A4/US-letter two-column template).
Section 1 preserves the template's guidance **as written**. Section 2 is the validation
procedure to run against a paper. Section 3 lists what IEEEtran (LaTeX) already handles so you
do not flag it.

Hard rule when applying this skill to a user's paper: **report findings; do not rewrite prose
unless the user asks.** Many items (page limit, number of keywords) are conference-specific and
the user's call-for-papers overrides the template.

---

## 1. The guidelines, as written in the template

### 1.1 Abstract
- "The abstract is limited to 150 words and cannot contain equations, figures, tables, or
  references."
- "It should concisely state what was done, how it was done, principal results, and their
  significance."
- Run-in heading "Abstract" is italic/bold followed by an em dash: *Abstract*—Text…

### 1.2 Index Terms
- "The author shall provide up to 5 keywords (in alphabetical order) to help identify the
  major topics of the paper."
- "The thesaurus of IEEE indexing keywords is posted at
  https://www.ieee.org/publications/services/thesaurus-access-page.html."

### 1.3 Ease of use / integrity of the template
- "All margins, column widths, line spaces, and text fonts are prescribed; please do not alter
  them."
- "The heading margin in this template measures proportionately more than is customary. This
  measurement and others are deliberate … Please do not revise any of the current designations."
- Template is tailored for US letter-sized paper (check the conference: some require A4).

### 1.4 Conference paper preparation
- "Conference papers are limited to a maximum of five pages." (**Template default — the
  conference CFP page limit overrides this.**)
- "Please use automatic hyphenation and check your spelling."
- "Be sure your sentences are complete and that there is continuity within your paragraphs."
- "Check the numbering of your graphics (figures and tables) and make sure that all
  appropriate references are included."

### 1.5 Abbreviations and acronyms
- "Define abbreviations and acronyms the first time they are used in the text, even after they
  have been defined in the abstract."
- "Abbreviations such as IEEE, SI, ac, dc, and rms do not have to be defined."
- "Do not use abbreviations in the title or section headings unless they are unavoidable."

### 1.6 Units
- "Metric units are preferred … the use of the International System of Units (SI Units) is
  advocated. This system includes a subsystem of units based on the meter, kilogram, second,
  and ampere (MKSA). U.S. Customary units, or British units, may be used as secondary units
  (in parentheses). An exception is when U.S. Customary units are used as identifiers in trade,
  such as 3.5-inch disk drive."
- "Avoid combining SI and U.S. Customary units, such as current in amperes and magnetic field
  in oersteds. … If you must use mixed units, clearly state the units for each quantity that
  you use in an equation."
- "Do not mix complete spellings and abbreviations of units: 'Wb/m2' or 'webers per square
  meter', not 'webers/m2'. Spell out units when they appear in text: '. . . a few henries',
  not '. . . a few H'."
- "Use a zero before decimal points: '0.25', not '.25'. Use 'cm3', not 'cc'."

### 1.7 Equations
- Type equations in Times New Roman or Symbol font only (Word); LaTeX math is acceptable.
- "Number equations consecutively. Equation numbers, within parentheses, are to position flush
  right, as in (1), using a right tab stop."
- "To make your equations more compact, you may use the solidus ( / ), the exp function, or
  appropriate exponents."
- "Italicize Roman symbols for quantities and variables, but not Greek symbols."
- "Use a long dash, as shown in (1), rather than a hyphen for a minus sign."
- "Punctuate equations with commas or periods when they are part of a sentence."
- "Be sure that the symbols in your equation have been defined before or immediately
  following the equation."
- "Use '(1)', not 'Eq. (1)' or 'equation (1)', except at the beginning of a sentence:
  'Equation (1) is . . .'"

### 1.8 Footnotes
- "Number footnotes separately in superscripts. Place the actual footnote at the bottom of the
  column in which it was cited."
- "Do not put footnotes in the reference list."
- "Use letters for table footnotes."
- Sponsor acknowledgments go in the unnumbered footnote on the first page.

### 1.9 Some common mistakes (verbatim list)
- The word "data" is plural, not singular.
- The subscript for the permeability of vacuum μ0, and other common scientific constants, is
  zero with subscript formatting, not a lowercase letter "o".
- In American English, commas, semi-/colons, periods, question and exclamation marks are
  located within quotation marks only when a complete thought or name is cited, such as a
  title or full quotation. When quotation marks are used, instead of a bold or italic typeface,
  to highlight a word or phrase, punctuation should appear outside of the quotation marks.
  A parenthetical phrase or statement at the end of a sentence is punctuated outside of the
  closing parenthesis (like this). (A parenthetical sentence is punctuated within the
  parentheses.)
- A graph within a graph is an "inset", not an "insert". The word alternatively is preferred
  to the word "alternately" (unless you really mean something that alternates).
- Do not use the word "essentially" to mean "approximately" or "effectively".
- In your paper title, if the words "that uses" can accurately replace the word "using",
  capitalize the "u"; if not, keep using lower-cased.
- Be aware of the different meanings of the homophones "affect" and "effect", "complement" and
  "compliment", "discreet" and "discrete", "principal" and "principle".
- Do not confuse "imply" and "infer".
- The prefix "non" is not a word; it should be joined to the word it modifies, usually without
  a hyphen.
- There is no period after the "et" in the Latin abbreviation "et al.".
- The abbreviation "i.e." means "that is", and the abbreviation "e.g." means "for example".

### 1.10 Authors and affiliations
- "Author affiliations are not repeated each time for multiple authors of the same
  affiliation. Please keep your affiliations as succinct as possible (for example, do not
  differentiate among departments of the same organization)."
- Template layout: Line 1 author name(s); Line 2 further names; Line 3 department; Line 4
  organization (acronyms acceptable); Line 5 City, Country; Line 6 e-mail if desired.
- Graphics: "ideally a 300 dpi TIFF or EPS file, with all fonts embedded."

### 1.11 Headings
- "There are two types: component headings and text headings."
- "Component headings identify the different components of your paper and are not topically
  subordinate to each other. Examples include ACKNOWLEDGMENTS and REFERENCES and, for these,
  the correct style to use is 'Heading 5'."
- "Use 'figure caption' for your Figure captions, and 'table heading' for your table title."
- "Text headings organize the topics on a relational, hierarchical basis. … If there are two
  or more sub-topics, the next level heading (uppercase Roman numerals) should be used and,
  conversely, if there are not at least two sub-topics, then no subheadings should be
  introduced."
- Heading levels: Heading 1 = I. SECTION (small caps, Roman numeral); Heading 2 = A. Subsection
  (italic); Heading 3 = 1) Sub-subsection (italic, run-in); Heading 4 = a) (italic, run-in).

### 1.12 Figures and tables
- "Large figures and tables may span across both columns."
- "Figure captions should be below the figures; table headings should appear above the
  tables."
- "Insert figures and tables after they are cited in the text as close to the citation as
  possible."
- "Use the abbreviation 'Fig. 1', even at the beginning of a sentence."
- Table title style: TABLE I. TITLE IN SMALL CAPS, centered above the table.
- "Figure Labels: Use 8 point Times New Roman for Figure labels. Use words rather than symbols
  or abbreviations when writing Figure axis labels to avoid confusing the reader. As an
  example, write the quantity 'Magnetization', or 'Magnetization, M', not just 'M'. If
  including units in the label, present them within parentheses. Do not label axes only with
  units. In the example, write 'Magnetization (A/m)' or 'Magnetization {A[m(1)]}', not just
  'A/m'. Do not label axes with a ratio of quantities and units. For example, write
  'Temperature (K)', not 'Temperature/K'."
- "Figures and tables should be numbered consecutively. Use Arabic numerals for figures and
  Roman numerals for tables."
- Figure caption format: "Fig. 1. Example of a figure caption." Table footnotes use
  superscript letters: "a. Example of a Table footnote."

### 1.13 Acknowledgment
- "The preferred spelling of the word 'acknowledgment' in America is without an 'e' after the
  'g'. Avoid the stilted expression, 'One of us (R. B. G.) thanks . . .' Instead, try
  'R. B. G. thanks'. Put sponsor acknowledgments in the unnumbered footnote on the first page."

### 1.14 References
- "References are important to the reader; therefore, each citation must be complete and
  correct. There is no editorial check on references; therefore, an incomplete or wrong
  reference will be published unless caught by a reviewer and will detract from the authority
  and value of the paper. References should be readily available publications."
- "List only one reference per reference number. If a reference is available from two sources,
  each should be listed as a separate reference."
- "The template will number citations consecutively within brackets [1]. The sentence
  punctuation follows the bracket [2]. Multiple references [3], [4] are each numbered with
  separate brackets [4]-[6]. Refer simply to the reference number, as in [7]—do not use
  'Ref. [7]' or 'reference [7]' except at the beginning of a sentence: 'Reference [7] was the
  first . . .'"
- "Unless there are six authors or more give all authors' names; do not use 'et al.'."
- "Papers that have not been published, even if they have been submitted for publication,
  should be cited as 'unpublished'."
- "Capitalize only the first word in a paper title, except for proper nouns and element
  symbols."
- "For papers published in translation journals, please give the English citation first,
  followed by the original foreign-language citation."
- "Papers that have been accepted for publication, but not yet published, should be cited as
  'in press'."
- Reference formats by type (from the template's samples):
  - **Periodical:** J. F. Fuller, E. F. Fuchs, and K. J. Roesler, "Influence of harmonics on
    power distribution system protection," *IEEE Trans. Power Delivery*, vol. 3, pp. 549-557,
    Apr. 1988.
  - **Online periodical:** R. J. Vidmar. (1992, Aug.). On the use of atmospheric plasmas as
    electromagnetic reflectors. *IEEE Trans. Plasma Sci.* [Online]. 21(3), pp. 876-880.

  - **Book:** E. Clarke, *Circuit Analysis of AC Power Systems*, vol. I. New York: Wiley, 1950,
    p. 81.
  - **Chapter in book:** G. O. Young, "Synthetic structure of industrial plastics," in

  - **Online book:** J. Jones. (1991, May 10). *Networks*. (2nd ed.) [Online]. Available:
    http://www.atm.com
  - **Technical report:** E. E. Reber, R. L. Mitchell, and C. J. Carter, "Oxygen absorption in

  - **Online report:** S. L. Talleen. (1996, Apr.). The Intranet Architecture: Managing

## 2. Validation procedure (LaTeX / IEEEtran)

Run these against the `.tex` and the compiled PDF. Report each as PASS / FLAG with the line
number. Never edit unless asked.

1. **Compile** (`tectonic` / `pdflatex`), then `grep -iE "overfull|undefined|multiply"` on the
   log. `pdfinfo | grep Pages` vs. the conference page limit.
2. **Abstract**: word count of the `abstract` environment (`≤ 150` per template; conference
   may differ); grep for `\cite`, `$`, `\ref` inside it.
3. **Index terms**: count entries (`≤ 5` per template); check alphabetical order.
4. **Headings**: `grep -nE '^\\(sub)?section\{'`; flag abbreviations, `&`, and any level with
   exactly one child subsection.
5. **Abbreviations**: for each all-caps token, find the first body occurrence (after the
   abstract) and confirm it is spelled out there. Exempt: IEEE, SI, ac, dc, rms.
6. **Units**: `grep -nE '[0-9] ?(GB|MB|KB|ms|s|Hz|m|kg)\b'` → SI, thin space (`\,`) consistent;
   `grep -nE '[^0-9.]\.[0-9]'` → leading zero; mixed spelled/abbreviated units.
7. **Equations**: numbered consecutively; cited as "(n)" not "Eq. (n)".
8. **Footnotes**: numbered superscripts in text; letters for table footnotes (IEEEtran papers
   commonly use † — flag as *template says letters*, note that reviewers rarely object).
9. **Common mistakes greps**: `data (is|was|has)`, `et\. al`, `Ref\. \[`, `reference \[`,
   `\bessentially\b`, `\balternately\b`, `\binsert\b`, `non-`, `(i\.e\.|e\.g\.)[^,]`,
   homophones (`affect|effect|discreet|discrete|principal|principle|imply|infer`).
   Quotation-mark punctuation: queries/phrases quoted for highlighting should have punctuation
   outside the closing quote.
10. **Figures/tables**: every `\begin{figure}`/`\begin{table}` is cited in text before it
    appears; `Fig.~n` (never "Figure n"); Roman numerals for tables; caption below figure /
    above table (`\caption` placement in source); `\label` after `\caption`; axis labels are
    words with units in parentheses, never a ratio like `Temp/K`; label font ≥ 8 pt.
11. **Acknowledgment**: spelled without the "e"; sponsors in the first-page footnote.
12. **References**: every `\bibitem` is `\cite`d and vice versa; numbers appear in first-use
    order; no "Ref. [n]"; all authors listed when < 6; paper titles sentence-case (first word
    + proper nouns only); every arXiv/online entry uses the same "[Online]. Available:" style;
    years match the source (check arXiv `published` date); one source per number.
13. **Title**: "using" vs "Using" rule; no abbreviations unless unavoidable.
14. **Final diff**: when the user allows fixes, snapshot first, then `diff` the PDF text
    (`pdftotext`) before vs. after and list every change.

## 3. Handled automatically by IEEEtran — do not flag

Margins, column widths, fonts, heading styles (small caps section titles, italic subsections),
caption font size and the "Fig. n." / "TABLE I" prefixes, abstract/index-terms run-in
headings, equation numbering flush right, citation brackets, two-column layout. Only flag
these if the author overrode them (`\setlength`, `\fontsize`, custom caption packages).
